Fig. 12 · the centerpiece

Splashery

An interactive Gaussian splat playground that runs entirely in the browser. Pick a toy (a real captured object, or one generated from a seed), spin it, poke it, blow on it, splash paint on it, drop it, watch it fall apart and come back, make your own, and share it as a link, an embed, a GIF or a video.

Play Splashery Code Tinkerer's Manual

My part: I defined the project's vision and its toy categories, refined the interaction design through hands-on testing, and contributed mathematical recipes.

Four ways Splashery lets you see sound

One

A Chladni plate whose sand moves live

Every grain moves on every frame: it hops more where the plate swings more, and slides toward the plate's still lines. The swing is a live mix of the plate's modes, each as strong as the sound drives it. Change the note and the sand sets off for the new figure from where it lies. Hold a note and it settles into a crisp figure. Silence leaves it where it is. Turn on the microphone and the note you sing drives the plate: the mode nearest your note rings, as strongly as you sing.

A second toy does it in three dimensions: beads in a small cell of water gather on the still surfaces of a standing wave, pushed there by the acoustic radiation force.

w(x, y) = cos(nπx)·cos(mπy) ± cos(mπx)·cos(nπy)

On this page: the plate at the top follows that model, and a figure's pitch follows n² + m², so every note is a whole multiple of the lowest. With n and m up to 4 it can make exactly 24 figures on 14 pitches. Here is every one, lowest note first. Tap any to ring it.

Two

A sound lab

A bench instrument with a scrolling spectrogram, the spectrum with its loudest frequency, and an oscilloscope. It shows the microphone once you tap "Use my microphone", and otherwise its own tone generator: sine, square, saw or noise, one tone or two that beat. Everything on its screens is measured from samples.

On this page: three screens, drawn in sand, that read this page's own sound. Loudest now: nothing yet.

Nothing of yours yet. Bow the plate or sing to it, then play it back.

Three

A song landscape

Open a song and Splashery cuts it into frames 40 milliseconds apart. Every shape it draws is a measured feature of the sound at that moment: its bands, its loudness, its spectral centroid (the brightness) and its pitch. The song plays as soon as it opens, and the picture fills in as the frames are measured.

On this page: a landscape of what you have played. One ridge per 40 millisecond frame, newest in front; a heavier line is a louder frame, the dot marks the measured pitch and the tick the centroid. The page keeps your last notes (which figure, how hard and when, never the sound), so Play it back draws them again.

Nothing of yours yet. Bow the plate or sing to it, then play it back.

Four

A synthesized sound for every toy

Every toy has its own tap sound, synthesized from a library of about 80 voices: plucked strings, bells, xylophone bars, drums, creature voices, wind, engines, horns. The guitar strums a chord progression, Big Ben plays the Westminster quarters, and the music box plays a melody. Sound is off until you turn it on.

On this page: every project below has a figure of its own and the note that makes it. Tap a small plate to ring it.

grooph

Visual loop graphs for coding-agent workflows. You draw who builds, who checks, where a person decides and when the work stops.

grooph checks that every loop names a stop, then compiles the graph into a prompt package for a separate coding-agent harness. It runs no agent and calls no model, and graphs stay on your device.
