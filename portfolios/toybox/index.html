What’s in the box

Splashery

Splats you can play with. A toy box of 3D Gaussian splats that runs entirely in the browser: pick a toy (a real captured object, or one generated from a seed), spin it, poke it, blow on it, splash paint on it, drop it, watch it fall apart and come back, make your own, and share it as a link, an embed, a GIF or a video.

The real thing, live: a captured cactus from Splashery’s shelf.

About 8 MB (the engine plus one scan), so it waits until you ask. Or open Splashery in its own tab.

My part

I defined the project’s vision and its toy categories, refined the interaction design through hands-on testing, and contributed mathematical recipes.

It is built on the PlayCanvas engine, with WebGPU when the browser has it and WebGL2 otherwise. Static files and ES modules only: no build step, no framework, no server.

  • Toys

    Real scans of up to 450,000 splats, and generated toys built in the browser from seeded recipes: sports balls, planets, food, a neuron that fires, a chess set that plays Morphy’s Opera Game.

  • Eight effects

    Poke, wind, dissolve, drop, magnet, twist, slice and paint. Each runs on the GPU for every splat, every frame, and they stack.

  • A sound for every toy

    Synthesized from a library of about 80 voices. The guitar strums a chord progression; Big Ben plays the Westminster quarters. Off until you turn it on.

  • Pictures and pages

    In the labs: open a PDF, a picture, a GIF or a video and see it made of splats. Photos get one splat per pixel; GIFs and videos recolor their splats every frame.

  • Six ways out

    A link with the whole scene compressed into it, a JSON file, a PNG, a GIF, a WebM video, or an embed: an iframe or a <splashery-toy> element.

  • A manual

    The Tinkerer’s Manual explains how to program splats, level by level, starting with what one splat is: the Gaussian and its covariance.

One splat, up close

The toy above is thousands of these: a flat Gaussian, brightest in the middle, switched off three sigmas out. Stretch it and turn it, and its covariance follows.

Σ = R S Sᵀ Rᵀ

Cutting at 3σ keeps 98.9% of a splat’s paint: 1 − e−4.5. I didn’t take the formula’s word for it; a script adds up 16,777,216 samples and gets 98.889%.
