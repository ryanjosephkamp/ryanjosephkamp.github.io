I’m a master’s student in computer science at the University of Cincinnati, with degrees in mathematics and neuroscience from the same place. What I want to make is splats that explain: pictures of science you can take apart until the thing makes sense.

The cell on the slide is the smallest example I could build. Turn the focus down and its 32,768 Gaussians merge, pair by pair, until the whole neuron is one soft blob with the same mass, center and spread it started with. Turn it back up and tap: the spike you see is the Hodgkin–Huxley equations, solved while you watch. Point at a label and its part lights up. Change the stain and the same splats take up different dyes.

Case I the collection

Splashery

An interactive Gaussian splat playground that runs entirely in the browser. Pick a toy, a real captured object or one generated from a seed, then spin it, poke it, blow on it, splash paint on it, drop it, watch it fall apart and come back, make your own, and share it as a link, an embed, a GIF or a video.

Among the donuts and rubber ducks it keeps science shelves, where the toys do what the real thing does. The drawers below hold some of them. Pull one onto the bench: it is the real toy, live.

What the science shelves are computed from

The drawers above are a sample. This is the longer catalog, by subject, with what each exhibit actually solves, samples or reads.

Waves and optics
A ripple tank that solves the 2D wave equation, with dippers, a plane wave, and one or two slits. A light bench that traces rays with Snell’s law through lenses, mirrors, a prism, a glass block and a fiber.
Light
Line spectra of sixteen elements from NIST data. A prism, with the glass’s index from its Sellmeier fit, and a grating, by d sin θ = mλ.
Sound
A Chladni plate whose sand moves live with the sound. A sound lab with a spectrogram, a spectrum and an oscilloscope. A song landscape drawn from measured features: bands, loudness, spectral centroid, pitch.
Molecules and crystals
A molecule viewer that opens PDB, mmCIF, SDF, MOL and XYZ files or fetches an entry from the Protein Data Bank, with distance and angle measurement. Crystal unit cells from published lattice constants. Hydrogen orbitals sampled from |ψ|².
Volumes and imaging
A volume viewer that opens CT, MRI and microscope scans on the device, with window and level, a transfer function, cut planes and a maximum-intensity picture. Its samples are a walnut and a gar’s head.
Scale and sky
Powers of ten: one continuous zoom from a galaxy down to a single molecular machine. The night sky for any date, time and place, with stars to magnitude 6 from the HYG database.
Earth and data
Real landscapes from public data: the Grand Canyon, Mount St. Helens before and after May 18, 1980, the Mariana Trench. A CSV becomes a scatter, bars or a surface. Mauna Loa CO2 as a spiral.
How splats work
A toy that fits 2D Gaussians to a picture by gradient descent, step by step, and shows one splat, many splats and sorting. A splat equation toy. The Tinkerer’s Manual: what one splat is, how a recipe is built, and the equation language.

My part

I defined the project’s vision and its toy categories, refined the interaction design through hands-on testing, and contributed mathematical recipes.

Toys
Captured ones are real scans of up to 450,000 splats. Generated ones are built from seeded recipes, so a saved scene rebuilds the same toy.
Effects
Poke, wind, dissolve, drop, magnet, twist, slice and paint, run on the GPU for every splat, every frame.
Sharing
A link, a JSON file, a PNG, a GIF, a WebM, or an embed. Bring your own splat file: PLY, SOG, SPLAT, SPZ.
Made of
Static files and ES modules only: no build step, no framework, no server. PlayCanvas engine, WebGPU with a WebGL2 fallback.

Drawer 1 software engineering

grooph

Visual loop graphs for coding-agent workflows. You draw who builds, who checks, where a person decides and when the work stops.

grooph checks that every loop names a stop, then compiles the graph into a prompt package for a separate coding-agent harness. It runs no agent and calls no model, and graphs stay on your device. The graph on this page is 8,192 splats running one such loop: around again while the bar is not met, then out to a person.
