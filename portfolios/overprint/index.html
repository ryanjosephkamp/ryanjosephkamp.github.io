01 Gaussian splats

        
Splashery

        
An interactive Gaussian splat playground.

        
It runs entirely in the browser. Pick a toy, a real captured object or one generated from a seed, then spin it, poke it, blow on it, splash paint on it, drop it, and watch it fall apart and come back. Make your own, and share it as a link, an embed, a GIF or a video.

        
On this page, the cloud beside this text is a small cousin of those toys: 96,000 splats, drawn by a renderer written for the page. Push the pointer through it, or drag to turn it.

        
I defined the project’s vision and its toy categories, refined the interaction design through hands-on testing, and contributed mathematical recipes.

        
On its shelves

        
    
          
  • Waves and optics. A ripple tank that solves the 2D wave equation, and a light bench that traces rays with Snell’s law through lenses, mirrors, a prism, a glass block and a fiber.
    • 
          
  • Sound. A Chladni plate whose sand moves live with the sound, and a song landscape drawn from measured features.
    • 
          
  • Molecules and crystals. A viewer that opens PDB, mmCIF, SDF, MOL and XYZ files or fetches an entry from the Protein Data Bank, and hydrogen orbitals sampled from |ψ|².
    • 
          
  • Volumes and imaging. CT, MRI and microscope scans opened on the device, with window and level, a transfer function and cut planes.
    • 
          
  • Scale and sky. One continuous zoom from a galaxy down to a single molecular machine, and the night sky for any date, time and place.
    • 
          
  • Earth and data. Real landscapes from public data, and a CSV turned into a scatter, bars or a surface.
    • 
          
  • Math. A Lorenz attractor, a Klein bottle and a gyroid among them. Those three are redrawn on this page.

01 Splashery, continued

        
Recipes

        
A toy that is written, not scanned.

        
Splashery’s captured toys are real scans of up to 450,000 splats. Its generated ones are built from seeded recipes, so a saved scene rebuilds the same toy. A splat equation toy goes further and places every splat from equations in u, v and t.

        
Its Tinkerer’s Manual explains what one splat is (the Gaussian and its covariance), how a recipe is built, and the equation language. Another toy fits 2D Gaussians to a picture by gradient descent, step by step.

        
On this page, every form is made the same way in miniature. A seeded recipe a few lines long says where each splat goes, which way its stroke runs, and which ink it takes. The card at the top of the page lets you change one, or write your own in u and v.

02 Graphs for agents

        
grooph

        
Visual loop graphs for coding-agent workflows.

        
You draw who builds, who checks, where a person decides and when the work stops. grooph checks that every loop names a stop, then compiles the graph into a prompt package for a separate coding-agent harness. It runs no agent and calls no model, and graphs stay on your device.

        
On this page, the figure is about the smallest such graph: a builder, a critic, one loop back, and a person at the stop.

03 Words, rearranged

        
Ars Magna

        
An anagram of “anagrams.”

        
An anagram finder. Give it any text and it returns every way those exact letters can be re-partitioned into real English words, by a deterministic search.

        
On this page, the figure spells the proof of its name: ANAGRAMS and ARS MAGNA.
