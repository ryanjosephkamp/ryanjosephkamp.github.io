Project 1 · 2026 to present

Splashery

16Ssulfur 15Pphosphorus 57Lalanthanum 16Ssulfur 1Hhydrogen 68Ererbium 39Yyttrium

An interactive Gaussian splat playground that runs entirely in the browser. Pick a toy (a real captured object, or one generated from a seed), spin it, poke it, blow on it, splash paint on it, drop it, watch it fall apart and come back, make your own, and share it as a link, an embed, a GIF or a video.

Its molecule viewer is the board above, made of splats in place of chalk. It opens PDB, mmCIF, SDF, MOL and XYZ files from your device, or fetches an entry from the Protein Data Bank by its code, and draws it as balls and sticks, space-filling atoms, a cartoon of its chains or its molecular surface. Tap two atoms and it measures the distance between them; tap a third and it measures the angle. The chalk board reads the same five kinds of file, and fetches by the same codes.

Beside the viewer sit crystal unit cells built from published lattice constants, and an atlas of hydrogen orbitals sampled from |ψ|². Both are redrawn in chalk below, so you can hold those too.

Play Splashery Play with the lab toys switched on Code Tinkerer's Manual

The molecule viewer, the unit cells and the orbital atlas are lab toys: the second link switches them on. Ryan defined the project's vision and its toy categories, refined the interaction design through hands-on testing, and contributed mathematical recipes.

  • Toys Captured ones are real scans of up to 450,000 splats. Generated ones are built from seeded recipes, so a saved scene rebuilds the same toy.
  • Effects Poke, wind, dissolve, drop, magnet, twist, slice and paint run on the GPU for every splat, every frame.
  • Also on its shelves A ripple tank, a light bench, line spectra of sixteen elements, a Chladni plate, a volume viewer for CT and MRI scans, the night sky for any date and place.
  • Engineering Static files and ES modules only: no build step, no framework, no server.

grooph

Visual loop graphs for coding-agent workflows. You draw who builds, who checks, where a person decides and when the work stops.

grooph checks that every loop names a stop, then compiles the graph into a prompt package for a separate coding-agent harness. It runs no agent and calls no model, and graphs stay on your device.

Bring your own

Four characters name an entry: 4HHB is human hemoglobin. Up to three name a chemical component: GTP. This asks rcsb.org for that one file.

PDB, mmCIF, SDF, MOL or XYZ. It is read here, on your device, and sent nowhere. You can also drop a file on the first board.
