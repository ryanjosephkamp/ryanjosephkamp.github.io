Definition. One splat

A splat is a Gaussian: a center μ and a covariance Σ. It is a soft ellipse with no edge, brightest in the middle and fading as exp(−½ d2), where d counts standard deviations from the center.

Σ = R S STRT = 2.010.720.720.60

R = 0.91−0.420.420.91 S = 1.50000.60 det Σ = 0.810

Renderers store Σ as a rotation R and a scale S because any pair of them gives a valid covariance, so an optimizer can move them freely. In three dimensions a splat gains a third axis, an opacity and a color; the renderer projects each one onto the screen, sorts them by depth and blends (Kerbl et al., 2023).

Splashery’s Tinkerer’s Manual teaches this level by level: what one splat is, how a recipe is built, and a splat equation language.

Lemma. Splitting loses nothing

However a splat’s pixels are divided, the two halves carry the parent’s mass, mean and covariance between them.

Σ = w1(Σ1 + δ1δ1T) + w2(Σ2 + δ2δ2T), δk = μk − μ

Each half brings two things: its own spread, Σk, and the offset of its center from the parent’s, δk. Weighted by its share of the pixels, wk, they add up to the parent again.

Proof. By checking. All 16,383 splits behind the picture at the top of this page hold, with a worst relative error of 0. A test suite checks 169,544 more on bars, rings, diagonals and dust, recomputes every node from its raw points, and tries 11,200 divisions that are not at the median: straight cuts at any angle, and divisions with no shape at all. ∎

The name is fitted by moment matching: each splat is the exact mean and covariance of the pixels it stands for, and each split falls at the median along the long axis. Real Gaussian splatting fits by gradient descent. Splashery has a toy that shows it, next.

Example. Splashery

Splats you can play with.

A playground of 3D Gaussian splats that runs entirely in the browser: captured objects of up to 450,000 splats, and generated ones built from seeded recipes. Every effect (poke, wind, dissolve, drop, magnet, twist, slice, paint) runs on the GPU for every splat, every frame; the CPU only sets a few uniforms.

My part: I defined the project’s vision and its toy categories, refined the interaction design through hands-on testing, and contributed mathematical recipes.

PlayCanvas engine · WebGPU, with WebGL2 fallback · static files, no build step
